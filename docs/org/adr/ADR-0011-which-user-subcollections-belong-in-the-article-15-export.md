# ADR-0011 — Which `users/{uid}` subcollections belong in the Article 15 export

- **Status:** DECIDED 2026-09-03 by Malin, question by question, and BUILT the same day.
  See "The decision" below, and the amendment at the end for a sixth collection found
  during the build.
- **Date:** 2026-09-02 (panel), 2026-09-03 (decision)
- **Ticket:** BUT-1992 (with BUT-1990 reviewed in the same panel)
- **Panel:** 11 roles, convened blind, routed by `tools/stakeholder_router.py`
  (`tier: full-panel`, `high_stakes_hits: functions/src/account/account-deletion-cascade.ts`)

## The question

BUT-1957 widened the account-deletion cascade with ten `users/{uid}` subcollections and gave
the DELETION half a source-derived drift guard. There is no matching guard for
**EXPORT ⊇ DELETION**, and five of the ten have live writers — `onboarding`, `ingredients`,
`rate_limits`, `counters`, `acquisition`. None of them is in the Art. 15 data export.

So today those five are erased when a user deletes their account, having never been
obtainable by that user first.

Two things were on the table: **build the guard**, and **decide, per collection, whether it
is exported or exempt**. The panel agreed on the first and did not agree on the second.

## Why nothing was built

Phase 1.4 of the sprint rulebook says an unresolved high-stakes conflict does not get built.
This one is unresolved in a way that matters: the disagreement is about *what a user is owed*,
not about how to implement it.

The Software Architect also made a structural point that stands independently of who is right
about the collections: shipping the guard and the per-collection decisions in one commit is
precisely how an exemption gets rubber-stamped by the person writing the guard that exists to
prevent rubber-stamping.

## Where the roles landed

**Privacy / DPO** — export `onboarding`, `ingredients`, `acquisition`, `settings`
(collection-wide) and `rate_limits` (counts only); exempt `counters` as derived tallies over
content the bundle already carries in full. Held that only the *depth* is the founder's call,
not whether personal data is covered at all.

**Legal Counsel** — `onboarding`, `ingredients` and `acquisition` are a **breach as shipped**:
each is uid-scoped content generated by or about that user, with no Art. 15(4) carve-out in
play. `acquisition` is the clearest case, being marketing-adjacent attribution data.
`rate_limits` and `counters` are arguable, not settled: Recital 63 tolerates withholding data
whose disclosure would undermine an anti-abuse measure, which reaches `rate_limits` and
reaches `counters` more weakly. Called that second part a legal opinion needing Malin's
sign-off to become policy.

**Product Manager** — ship `ingredients` (plainly the user's own library) and `onboarding`
(cheap, closes the guard). Exempt `rate_limits` and `counters`: zero user value, rows a user
cannot interpret, pure bundle bloat. Cut `acquisition` from the sprint pending Malin, on the
grounds that it may read as surprising rather than as a win. A bigger export is not
automatically a better one.

**Trust & Safety** — `rate_limits` IS an abuse control (`{lastWrite}` per gated operation);
exporting it hands the subject their own cooldown reset time. Recommended redacting
`lastWrite` if it ships. Found no third-party data in any of the five.

**Security Architect** — measured the rules, which removed the objection everyone expected to
be the blocker: all five collections are already client-readable by their owner, so **no
`firestore.rules` change is needed** to export them. Also refuted T&S on `rate_limits`: the
limits themselves live in client code and are inferable from the 429 copy the user already
sees, so nothing secret is disclosed. Flagged one real caveat — `counters` accepts cross-user
increments, so its values are partly other people's actions; export the totals, never a
per-actor breakdown.

**DBA** — no composite index is needed and `firestore.indexes.json` must not be touched
(a `--force` deploy has destroyed live TTL policies here before). Rejected
`ExportResourceType` as the guard's anchor: it has fewer members than the repository has export
methods, the mapping is many-to-one, and its values are log labels rather than paths — someone adding a section reuses an existing
member and the guard passes green. The sound anchor is the `.doc(userId).collection(X)` chain
in the repository itself.

**Software Architect** — the guard must live in the existing TypeScript test beside
`scenario_everyUserSubcollectionHasADeleter`, reusing its resolver rather than
re-implementing it in Dart. Named the residual that matters: that suite runs on a
`functions/src` diff, while the mutation this guard catches is usually **Dart-only**, so the
guard is asleep for exactly the change most likely to break it unless the gate is wired
explicitly.

**FinOps** — immaterial. One export already issues ~39 queries with a documented ~60,000-read
worst case on the poll-vote leg; five more bounded reads are a rounding error. Removing the
dead `fcm_tokens` read saves exactly one minimum-charge read — trivial, and worth doing for
hygiene, not for money.

**Data Analyst / BI** — `acquisition` discloses only the user's own attribution stamp, no
spend and no partner identity; safe to export. Raised a separate defect: it has no aggregate
sibling, so every deletion silently removes that install channel from any future analysis.
Filed as BUT-1997.

**Performance Engineer** — cap every new section explicitly; `ingredients` is the only one
that grows with use.

**Vendor / Procurement** — no material stake, stated as a rubber stamp rather than an invented
concern.

## What everyone agreed on

- The missing guard is the real defect; the individual gaps are its symptoms.
- The guard must derive its universe from **source**, never a hand-written list — a
  hand-written list is what produced BUT-1957's own two gaps.
- Every exemption carries a written reason, and the bundle's own `data_minimisation` text must
  say what it does not contain. A bundle that misdescribes itself is its own Art. 12(1)
  defect — the precedent is BUT-1971.
- `settings` is asymmetric today (deletion takes the whole collection, export reads one
  document by id) and should be fixed by widening the export, not by narrowing the deletion.
- **BUT-1990 is not contested**: the `users/{uid}/fcm_tokens` reader was born dead and should
  go, while `fcm_tokens` stays in the cascade so an old account's rows are still swept. That
  half shipped in this sprint.

## The decision

**Malin, 2026-09-03**, asked one question at a time, each with the panel's split laid out.

| Collection | Decision | Whose view it follows |
|---|---|---|
| `ingredients` | **EXPORT** | unanimous |
| `onboarding` | **EXPORT** | unanimous |
| `acquisition` | **EXPORT, unprojected** | Legal, DPO, Data Analyst — over PM's "cut it pending Malin" |
| `rate_limits` | **EXEMPT** | PM — over DPO's "export counts only" |
| `counters` | **EXEMPT** | PM and DPO |

Two of the three answers took the recommendation; the third did not, and that is the one
worth recording carefully.

**On `acquisition`**, she was shown the strongest legal claim in the panel (Art. 15(1)(a)+(g),
the right to know the SOURCE of one's data) against the product worry that it reads as
surprising, plus the Data Analyst's measurement that the row holds only source, medium,
campaign and a first-seen stamp — no spend, no partner identity, nothing about anyone else.
She chose to export it as it stands. Do not projection-strip the campaign name later without
reopening this entry: "it sounds bad" was explicitly considered and rejected as a reason.

**On `rate_limits` she went against the recommendation**, which was to export it. The
recommendation rested on Security's measurement that nothing secret is disclosed — the limits
live in client code and are already visible in the throttling copy the user sees. That
measurement stands and was not disputed; she simply weighed bundle legibility higher than
completeness for a row that is one timestamp per gated operation. Legal had already said it
would accept an exemption as policy but would not author one, so this is the policy, and it
is hers.

**Both exemptions carry an obligation**: the bundle's own `data_minimisation` text must say
Butlery holds them. An exemption the data subject cannot see is not a minimisation decision,
it is an undisclosed gap (Art. 12(1), on the BUT-1971 precedent). The Art. 30 register gets a
row for all five either way — Art. 30 records processing, not export status.

The `settings` asymmetry is unchanged by these answers and still resolves the way the panel
agreed: widen the export to the whole collection, never narrow the deletion.

## What Malin had to decide (the questions as put to her)

1. **`acquisition`** — does the export include install attribution (which channel and campaign
   brought you in, and when), or is that internal growth plumbing? Legal calls withholding it
   a breach; PM thinks users may find it surprising rather than welcome.
2. **`rate_limits` and `counters`** — permanently exempt as plumbing, with a line in the
   bundle saying Butlery holds them? PM and (for `counters`) DPO say yes. Legal will accept it
   as policy but not author it.
3. **`onboarding` and `ingredients`** — everyone who expressed a view says export. Confirming
   that is a formality, but it is the formality that lets the guard go green.

## AMENDED 2026-09-03, during the build — a SIXTH live-writer collection

This record says "five of the ten have live writers". That was true of the panel's universe
and is the count the questions were put to Malin against. The build found a sixth:
`report_throttle`, written by `firebase_report_repository.dart`, in `subs`, erased on account
deletion, and not exported.

It was exempted **without asking her** — it was not among the three questions — on the
reasoning that the reports themselves ARE exported (`reports` where `reporterId == uid`), so
the throttle adds only a derived recency stamp on top of rows the subject already receives.
Its doc id is the REPORTED user's uid, so exporting it would also ship a third-party
identifier the reports already carry in a field.

It is named in the bundle's `data_minimisation` text like the two she decided, so the data
subject can see it is held.

**Malin closed BUT-1992 on 2026-09-03 having been told this**, so the exemption is SEEN AND
ACCEPTED as delivered — deliberately a weaker statement than the five above, which she
weighed one at a time against the panel's split. She was not asked to judge this one on its
merits, and widening or reversing it remains hers.

The decision lines above are unchanged; this amendment records what was found after them.

## Consequences of leaving it open

The gap is live and grows with every future widening of the cascade — which is the argument
for High priority on BUT-1992, and the reason this is written down rather than carried in a
sprint report nobody re-reads.
